2. PURPOSE OF THIS AFFIDAVIT: I provide this affidavit based on my personal,

firsthand knowledge of events that occurred on March 2, 2026, the day Conrad Alan
Rockenhaus was released from federal custody. This affidavit describes what I personally

observed, what Petitioner communicated to me directly on that day, and what I personally said

and did in response.

3. ARRIVAL AT 26695 ROSS DRIVE ON MARCH 2, 2026: On March 2, 2026, I went to

26695 Ross Drive, Redford Township, Michigan, after learning that Conrad had been released

from federal custody and had returned to that address. When I arrived, Conrad was present at

the house. It was clear to me that Conrad had just been released from custody earlier that day.

He appeared confused and disoriented, which I understood to be a reasonable reaction to

having just been released from federal prison.

4. PETITIONER'S INSTRUCTIONS BEFORE REDFORD POLICE ARRIVED: Before

the Redford Township Police arrived at the scene, Petitioner communicated with me and

instructed me to tell the Veterans Administration that Conrad was suicidal. Based on my own

direct observation of Conrad that day, this statement was not true. Conrad was not suicidal. I

did not believe Conrad was suicidal, and I told Petitioner so. Petitioner nonetheless continued

to insist that I should make this statement to the Veterans Administration.

5. REDFORD TOWNSIDP POLICE RESPONSE: The Redford Township Police

Department arrived at the scene in response to Petitioner's call. The responding officer read the

conditions of a Personal Protection Order to Conrad from Petitioner's cell phone screen. I was

personally present and witnessed this. Conrad was not handed a physical copy of the Personal

Protection Order by the officer, by Petitioner, or by anyone else. At one point, Conrad's

belongings could not be located, and photographs of the Personal Protection Order were taken

with my cell phone so that Conrad would have something to refer to. These photographs were

the only written form of the order Conrad had on his person when he left the scene.
