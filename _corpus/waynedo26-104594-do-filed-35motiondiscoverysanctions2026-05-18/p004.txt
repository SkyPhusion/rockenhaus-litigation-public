II. THE RFA ANSWERS ARE INSUFFICIENT AS A MATTER OF LAW
                                              A. Legal Standard
Under MCR 2.312(B)(1), a party served with a Request for Admission must, within twenty-eight days, serve
a written answer or objection. The Rule prescribes the form of a permissible answer:

          The answer must specifically deny the matter or state in detail the reasons why the answering
          party cannot truthfully admit or deny the matter. A denial must fairly meet the substance of the
          requested admission, and when good faith requires that a party qualify an answer or deny only
          a part of the matter of which an admission is requested, the party shall specify so much of it as
          is true and qualify or deny the remainder. An answering party may not give lack of
          information or knowledge as a reason for failure to admit or deny unless the party states
          that he or she has made reasonable inquiry and that the information known or readily
          obtainable is insufficient to enable the party to admit or deny.

MCR 2.312(B)(1) (emphasis added).
Under MCR 2.312(B)(3), the propounding party "may move to determine the sufficiency of the answers or
objections":

          Unless the court determines that an objection is justified, it shall order that an answer be
          served. If the court determines that an answer does not comply with the requirements of
          this rule, it may order either that the matter is admitted or that an amended answer be
          served. The court may, in lieu of these orders, determine that final disposition of the request be
          made at a pretrial conference or at a designated time prior to trial.

MCR 2.312(B)(3) (emphasis added).
The "reasonable inquiry" qualifier in MCR 2.312(B)(1) is not a magic phrase. A party who recites the words
"reasonable inquiry" without conducting any inquiry, or who recites the phrase as boilerplate for matters that
are within the party's own personal knowledge, has not satisfied the rule. The rule "imposes upon a party a
positive duty to make reasonable inquiry concerning matters in which the responding party may not currently
have direct knowledge but which are reasonably within his power to ascertain." Medbury v Walsh, 190 Mich
App 554, 556; 476 NW2d 470 (1991). When the matter is within the party's own personal knowledge, no
inquiry is required, and the boilerplate recitation is dispositively insufficient.

                                 B. Application: Every Answer Is Identical
Plaintiff/Counter-Defendant served fifty-three Answers to Defendant's Requests for Admissions on May 13,
2026. The substantive answer to each of the fifty-three Requests, without variation, is:

          Plaintiff cannot truthfully admit nor deny for lack of information or knowledge. Plaintiff has made
          reasonable inquiry and the information known or readily obtainable is insufficient at this time to
          enable Plaintiff to admit or deny the request for admission.

The verbatim formula appears as the substantive answer to RFA Nos. 1 through 53 inclusive. There is no
qualification. There is no partial admission. There is no specific denial. There is no statement explaining
what "reasonable inquiry" was made or why the matter is not within Plaintiff/Counter-Defendant's personal
knowledge.

satisfy MCR 2.312(B)(1). It also strains credulity. The Requests cover discrete factual matters about
