                                  C. Specific Examples of Facial Insufficiency
The insufficiency is most clearly visible in the categories of Requests that concern matters obviously and
exclusively within Plaintiff/Counter-Defendant's own knowledge:
Visits to FCI Milan (RFAs 1-4). Whether Plaintiff/Counter-Defendant visited Defendant at a federal
correctional institution at any time is a binary historical fact known to her. The Bureau of Prisons maintains
visitation logs that are obtainable by Plaintiff/Counter-Defendant upon request. The answer "cannot truthfully
admit nor deny for lack of information" to "You visited Conrad Alan Rockenhaus at FCI Milan on at least one
occasion" is facially insufficient.
Correspondence to and from Defendant (RFAs 5-9). Whether Plaintiff/Counter-Defendant received letters
from Defendant during his incarceration, whether she sent him a handwritten letter in or about November
2025, whether she sent him Priority Mail Express packages, and whether she forwarded a particular
envelope to the Robert J. Delonis Center after the PPO was filed are facts about her own actions. The post
offices, the Bureau of Prisons mail logs, and her own household are obvious sources of "reasonable
inquiry." The identical boilerplate is insufficient.
TRULINCS messages (RFAs 10-13). Whether Plaintiff/Counter-Defendant communicated with Defendant
via the federal Bureau of Prisons electronic messaging system, whether she sent messages containing
specifically quoted phrases such as "I love you," "Hey Baby," and "I miss you like crazy," and whether she
sent a message on October 28, 2025 concerning Defendant's VA pay deposit, are facts entirely within her
personal knowledge. TRULINCS messages are also preserved by the Bureau of Prisons and obtainable by
Plaintiff/Counter-Defendant. The boilerplate is insufficient.
Sworn federal court declarations (RFAs 14-17). Whether Plaintiff/Counter-Defendant filed sworn
declarations in Rockenhaus v. Agapiou, Case No. 2:25-cv-12716 (E.D. Mich.) identifying herself as
Defendant's wife and next friend, whether she filed nine or more such declarations between September
2025 and January 2026, whether she filed a Notice of Appeal in Rockenhaus v. Pringle, Case No. 2:25-cv-
12913 (E.D. Mich.) on or about January 21, 2026, and whether she described herself in those filings as
Defendant's devoted wife and advocate, are facts about her own sworn filings in federal court. PACER,
accessible to any member of the public, returns the filings on a one-minute search. To claim "lack of
information or knowledge" of her own federal court filings, on which she swore under penalty of perjury, is
impermissible.
Published blog and social media content (RFAs 18-22). Whether Plaintiff/Counter-Defendant published
blog posts on rockenhaus.com advocating for Defendant's release, whether she posted content on X
(Twitter), Facebook, and Threads on the same subject, whether she redirected her personal domain
adezero.com to rockenhaus.com, whether she posted a Valentine's Day message of affection directed at
Defendant on or about February 14, 2026, and whether she published content on rockenhaus.com,
fitspo.net, or lawflaws.com after the PPO was filed, are facts about her own authorship of her own published
content. Plaintiff/Counter-Defendant controls the domains, the hosting accounts, and the social media
accounts in question. The boilerplate is insufficient.
Financial accounts and tax filings (RFAs 23-28). Whether Plaintiff/Counter-Defendant received VA
Disability Compensation payments belonging to Defendant into a bank account under her control between
September 2025 and February 2026, whether she received SSDI benefit payments belonging to Defendant
