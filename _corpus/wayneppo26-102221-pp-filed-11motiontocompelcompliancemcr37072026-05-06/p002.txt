                   III. ADDITIONAL HARM SUPPORTING IMMEDIATE COMPLIANCE
The mandatory nature of MCR 3.707(A)(2) is, standing alone, sufficient to require immediate scheduling of the merits
hearing. The harms set forth in this Section are presented as additional support, not as a substitute for the rule-
compliance ground.

                                  A. Deprivation of VA-Prescribed Medical Device
14. Respondent is a one hundred percent (100%) service-connected disabled combat veteran. Service-connected
    disabilities include traumatic brain injury, seizure disorder, and other conditions for which Respondent receives
    ongoing treatment through the Department of Veterans Affairs.
15. Among the medical devices prescribed by the VA as part of Respondent's treatment regimen is an electrical-
    stimulation (e-stim) unit. This device is an integral component of an established, VA-supervised therapy protocol.
16. The e-stim device is presently held at the marital residence located at the property to which the Personal Protection
    Order bars Respondent's access. Respondent has been deprived of access to this VA-prescribed medical device
    since his release from federal custody on March 2, 2026, a period of sixty-five (65) days as of the filing of this
    Motion.
17. Deprivation of a VA-prescribed medical device is not equivalent to deprivation of ordinary personal property. It is
    interference with a clinician-supervised treatment regimen for service-connected medical conditions. The clinical
    impact of sixty-five days without prescribed therapy is ongoing and accumulating.
18. The underlying VA prescription is on file with the Department of Veterans Affairs and is available for verification
    by the Court should verification be required.
