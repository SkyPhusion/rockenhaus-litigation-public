PREMARITAL DOMAIN ROCKENHAUS.COM
17. rockenhaus.com is Defendant's premarital separate property. It was registered on September 7, 2001,
    more than twenty-one years before the parties' marriage on May 20, 2023. WHOIS records confirm the
    creation date conclusively. Under MCL 552.401, property acquired before marriage is separate property
    not subject to equitable division.
18. The domain is currently registered through Cloudflare, Inc. and expires on September 7, 2026, less than
    five months from the date of this motion. This divorce proceeding is unlikely to reach final judgment
    before that date. Without a TRO, Plaintiff could allow the domain to lapse, transfer it to a third party, or
    otherwise permanently destroy this premarital asset before this Court adjudicates ownership.
19. Plaintiff is currently in possession and control of rockenhaus.com. She published blog posts on the site
    as recently as March 8, 2026. In her PPO petition filed February 20, 2026, she falsely swore under oath
    that rockenhaus.com was her own business domain and sought court orders prohibiting Defendant from
    accessing it. This sworn false statement establishes both her awareness of the domain's value and her
    intent to claim it as her own.
20. Defendant has no adequate remedy at law. A domain name, once transferred, deleted, or allowed to
    lapse, cannot be restored. Twenty-five years of associated identity, history, and digital presence will be
    permanently and irreversibly destroyed. Money damages cannot compensate for the loss of a unique
    premarital asset with a twenty-five year history.

21. All four factors for injunctive relief under Pontiac Fire Fighters Union Local 376 v. City of Pontiac, 482
    Mich 1 (2008) weigh decisively in Defendant's favor:
    a. Likelihood of success: The creation date of September 7, 2001 establishes premarital ownership as a
    matter of objective fact. WHOIS records are attached as Exhibit A.
    b. Irreparable harm: The domain expires September 7, 2026. Once lapsed or transferred, it is gone
    permanently.
    c. Balance of hardships: The TRO asks only that Plaintiff maintain the status quo and not permanently
    dispose of property she does not own. Zero hardship to Plaintiff, total and permanent harm to Defendant
    without it.
    d. Public interest: Michigan courts have a strong interest in protecting premarital separate property from
    dissipation during divorce proceedings.
